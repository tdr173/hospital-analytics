Dataset & Features

Real EHRs are GDPR-locked, so Synthea generated a synthetic cohort with deep longitudinal histories across 10+ relational tables (encounters, conditions, medications, procedures, immunizations, allergies, observations, care plans, providers, organizations).

K-Prototypes Clustering

K-Prototypes (Huang, 1998) extends K-Means to mixed data: squared-Euclidean distance for numerics plus matching dissimilarity for categoricals, combined in one distance.

  • An IQR rule keeps 21,995 patients for model fitting; the 289 high-acuity outliers are retained as a separate scope.
  • Benchmarked 63 feature versions × a sweep over scaler, γ, seed, threshold and k ∈ {4..7}, scored on 6 cluster-quality indices.
  • The Gower silhouette is weighted highest, the only index that honours the mixed metric; FAMD projections act as tie-breakers.
  • Final pick v55 (k=4): Gower 0.350, with all four clusters above the 0.20 cohesion heuristic.
  • Top drivers: active conditions (η²=0.74), age (0.66); condition burden (V=0.80), diabetes (0.69).

Sequence Mining

Each patient's history becomes one chronological sequence of events typed by prefix (C:condition, M:medication, P:procedure, CP:care plan). PrefixSpan (Pei et al., 2001) mines frequent ordered sub-sequences by prefix-projection, growing a pattern one event at a time and recursing only on the suffixes that follow, so it never enumerates candidates. It runs once per cluster, so each phenotype reveals its own trajectory.

  • Sequences are cleaned first: noise blacklisted, refills collapsed, a 730-day window and length cap applied.
  • Closed patterns are ranked by geometric-mean lift, which normalises by each item's baseline frequency so rare, meaningful chains beat high-support trivia.
  • A type-diversity bonus favours condition → medication → procedure chains; Jaccard 0.80 dedup and dental caps remove the rest, then survivors are attributed back to their patients.

AI Interpretation

Large language models turn the platform's statistical output into language that non-technical staff can read and act on, bridging numbers to decisions.

  • Cluster summaries: each cluster's statistics become a title, a narrative description, a risk level and a list of recommended interventions.
  • Text-to-SQL agent on LangGraph: a question such as "patients with >3 emergencies this year" becomes SQL, is executed, and is answered in natural language.
  • The generated SQL stays visible as a transparency safeguard, so staff can verify exactly what ran against the data.
  • Outputs are strictly descriptive, never medical advice or a substitute for a clinician.
  • Three roles (patient, doctor, manager) each see a tailored slice of the same data.

Key Findings & Impact

  1. The cohorts are real, not artefacts. Mixed clustering splits the hospital into four recognisable phenotypes along a morbidity gradient, each cohesive enough to clear the silhouette bar.
  2. Each cohort tells its own story. Mining per cluster turns every phenotype into a signature trajectory (a child's ear infection, an antenatal work-up, a heart-failure rescue) that a single global run would average away.
  3. Ranking by lift makes them readable. Geometric-mean lift rewards rare, meaningful chains and suppresses Synthea's 40%-support dental noise, so what surfaces is clinical, not bureaucratic.
  4. The rarest patients matter most. Mining the 289 outliers as their own scope exposes the high-cost oncology and renal chains that fall below any global support floor.
  5. And it reaches people. Clinicians see where a patient fits and the pathway their cohort follows; managers query the hospital in plain language, served fast from offline batch jobs persisted to PostgreSQL.
