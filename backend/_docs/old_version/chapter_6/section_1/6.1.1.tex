\subsection{Sequence Mining Results}
\label{ch:results_sequences}

PrefixSpan (Section~\ref{ch:prefix_span_theory}, pipeline in Section~\ref{ch:pattern_mining_design}) was applied per scope: each of the four clinical clusters from Section~\ref{ch:results_clustering} produced its own pattern set, and the cohort of IQR-flagged outliers (\texttt{cluster\_id = -1}) was mined separately as a fifth pseudo-cluster, since these patients carry exactly the high-acuity signal that k-prototypes was designed to discard. The mining produced 175--252 frequent patterns per scope; we report the \textbf{top 15} of each, ranked by geometric mean lift (\texttt{gml}), which is the metric that promotes patterns whose every step is unusually frequent within the scope rather than merely globally common. Each row in the tables below carries the support count (\texttt{pts}), its share of the scope (\texttt{\%}), the \texttt{gml} score, and the ordered itemset using the prefix convention \texttt{C:}~condition, \texttt{M:}~medication, \texttt{P:}~procedure, \texttt{CP:}~careplan; the \texttt{[dental]} tag marks patterns flagged as dental-only by the post-mining filter. Long generic drug names are abbreviated to their international nonproprietary names for legibility.

\subsubsection*{Per-Cluster Reading}

Looking across the five tables, each scope produces a clinically distinct dominant signal:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cluster~0 (young / low-burden).} The strongest patterns are episodic acute care: laceration~$\to$~suture, fracture~$\to$~immobilization, acute-bronchitis~$\to$~acetaminophen, plus the universal dental fluoride / gingivitis bundle. No chronic-disease chain rises to the top -- consistent with the cluster's near-zero \texttt{active\_conditions\_count} from Section~\ref{ch:results_clustering}.
    \item \textbf{Cluster~1 (women of reproductive age).} Reproductive-health pathways are the discriminating signal: the tubal-ligation chain (rank~2--4, gml 0.81--0.92) and the normal-pregnancy~$\to$~ultrasound viability chain (rank~11). The cluster shares the acute-bronchitis and dental bundles with cluster~0 but is uniquely defined by these female-only sequences.
    \item \textbf{Cluster~2 (hypertensive adults, polypharmacy).} Defined by chronic-disease management: lisinopril paired with depression and substance-use screening (rank~14--15), furosemide / prone-positioning / oxygen sequences for fluid overload (rank~2, 10--12), the renal-dialysis / Epoetin Alfa loop (rank~3), and obstructive-airway pulmonary rehabilitation (rank~6--7). The combination of dental referrals \emph{with} antihypertensives (rank~14) is a marker of the integrated preventive bundle this cluster receives.
    \item \textbf{Cluster~3 (pediatric / acute).} Trauma- and allergy-dominated: acute allergic reaction~$\to$~epinephrine~$\to$~prednisone (rank~1, gml 1.00), epinephrine auto-injector education (rank~3), fracture chains for clavicle, forearm, ankle, bone (rank~4, 6), seizure / EEG (rank~7), and concussion rehabilitation (rank~10). Otitis-media antibiotic chains appear further down the long tail (rank 27+ in the source file).
    \item \textbf{Outliers (cluster~$-1$).} The high-acuity tail that the IQR filter pushed out of clustering surfaces here in concentrated form: chemotherapy + radiation with cisplatin / paclitaxel and a hospice-care follow-on (rank~3, 5, 6, 9--11, 13--14), renal dialysis with Epoetin Alfa (rank~7), kidney transplantation with tacrolimus (rank~4), and a heart-failure loop of furosemide + prone-position + oxygen (rank~1). Treating outliers as a separate scope is what makes these otherwise rare but high-cost pathways visible at all -- they are below the global minimum-support floor when mined against the whole cohort.
\end{itemize}

Taken together, the five pattern sets confirm that the $k{=}4$ partition is not only metrically defensible (Section~\ref{ch:results_clustering}) but also \emph{semantically} separable: each cluster's top patterns describe a recognisably different care population, which is the precondition for cluster-scoped pathway analytics in the dashboard.
