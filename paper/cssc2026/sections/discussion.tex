\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

Several limitations follow directly from the design choices. First, the cohort is
\emph{synthetic}: Synthea produces statistically plausible but not real
histories, so the discovered clusters and trajectories validate the
\emph{pipeline}, not clinical hypotheses. Transfer to real EHRs would require
re-tuning the feature definitions and re-validating with domain experts. Second,
clustering and sequence mining run as \emph{offline batch} jobs; the platform
does not recompute analytics in real time, which is acceptable for retrospective
analysis but not for live decision support. Third, the LLM layer is an
\emph{interpretation and access} aid: its summaries describe statistics and its
text-to-SQL output is validated, but generated text is not a medical
recommendation and the validation rules, while strict, are heuristic rather than
a formal guarantee. Finally, the role-based views implement scoping at the query
level rather than as a production-grade authorization module.

These boundaries are deliberate: the contribution is the integrated,
interpretation-first pipeline and the dual-scope mining idea, both of which are
independent of the synthetic data source and would carry over to a real
deployment.
