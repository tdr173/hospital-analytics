Summary of Dropped Tables and Attributes

The original Synthea dataset contains a large number of CSV files and attributes designed for simulation completeness, not for efficient relational storage or analysis.
To obtain a clean PostgreSQL database in Third Normal Form (3NF), several tables and attributes were intentionally excluded.

The selection was guided by the following principles:

avoid data redundancy

remove simulation-specific metadata

keep only attributes with analytical or clinical relevance

maintain a compact schema (8–10 tables)

1. Dropped Tables
payers

Reason for removal:

Contains insurance company information used only for billing simulation

Not relevant for medical analysis, patient clustering, or clinical history

Introduces dependencies unrelated to healthcare events

Conclusion:
Dropped because it does not contribute to medical or analytical objectives.

payer_transitions

Reason for removal:

Tracks insurance changes over time

Highly specific to the Synthea simulator

Not required for patient health modeling or medical outcomes

Conclusion:
Dropped due to low analytical value and strong simulator coupling.

supplies

Reason for removal:

Represents consumable items (gloves, syringes, materials)

Not clinically relevant at patient level

Strongly overlaps with procedures and encounters

Conclusion:
Dropped to avoid unnecessary low-level operational data.

imaging_studies

Reason for removal:

Largely overlaps with procedures and observations

Very sparse and inconsistent across patients

Adds complexity without increasing analytical insight

Conclusion:
Dropped to reduce redundancy and simplify the schema.

devices

Reason for removal:

Represents implanted or temporary medical devices

Low frequency in the dataset

Limited impact on patient-level analysis

Conclusion:
Dropped due to low usage and weak analytical relevance.

careplans

Reason for removal:

Abstract and high-level planning data

Not directly linked to measurable outcomes

Often duplicates information found in conditions and procedures

Conclusion:
Dropped to maintain a concrete, event-based data model.

2. Dropped Attributes from Kept Tables
Patients

Dropped attributes include:

exact address (street level)

latitude / longitude

simulation identifiers

internal Synthea metadata

Reason:

Not required for medical analysis

Violates minimal data principles

Geographic aggregation (city/state) is sufficient

Encounters

Dropped attributes include:

cost fields

payer references

detailed billing codes

duplicate organization or provider names

Reason:

Financial data is outside application scope

Redundant information already normalized via foreign keys

Medications / Procedures / Conditions

Dropped attributes include:

internal UUIDs used only by Synthea

textual duplicates of patient or encounter data

simulation timing metadata

Reason:

These attributes depend on non-key fields

Keeping them would violate 3NF

Observations

Dropped attributes include:

raw JSON values

reference ranges

device metadata

Reason:

Value + unit + code is sufficient

Extra metadata adds complexity without analytical benefit

3. Benefits of Dropping These Elements

By removing the above tables and attributes, the final database:

strictly follows Third Normal Form

avoids transitive dependencies

minimizes redundancy

improves query performance

is easier to maintain and extend

matches real-world EMR relational designs

4. Final Result

The resulting schema contains 10 carefully selected tables:

patients

providers

organizations

encounters

conditions

medications

procedures

immunizations

observations

allergies

This structure preserves all clinically relevant information while eliminating unnecessary simulation and billing data.
